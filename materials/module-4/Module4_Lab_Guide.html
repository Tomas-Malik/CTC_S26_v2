Search & Verify: Ground It, Extract It, Summarize It

Your step-by-step guide for all three beats. Work at your own laptop, alongside your AI assistant — this page tells you what to do, not what to type.

Level 1 No prerequisite Bring a laptop + AI assistant

“AI search doesn’t remove the work of finding things out — it relocates it. Your job stops being ‘search for the answer’ and becomes ‘verify what it found, and notice what it couldn’t have found.’”

The setup: your friend Priya is applying to UIC’s Computer Science PhD program and keeps getting different answers about the requirements from different places — a forum post, a chatbot, her own guessing. You offered to help her get it straight, using an AI assistant and the actual, current, official UIC documents — not a fictional case file this time. Priya isn’t carrying any facts of her own to track; every number in this module is real and traces back to one of the two documents below.

  • UIC CS Graduate Program Requirements (2025 edition) → The core grounding document for all three beats — covers both the MS and PhD programs. Download this before Beat One.
  • UIC Academic Catalog — PhD in Computer Science → Source of the one table used in Beat Two (English proficiency test scores). Have this open in a tab.

Beat One — Ground it

Goal: feel the difference between an AI assistant answering from memory and one answering from the actual document.

  1. Ask first, before you give it anything
    Open a fresh AI chat with no documents attached. Ask: “How many total credit hours does a UIC Computer Science PhD require, and by when do I need to pass my qualifying exam?”
  2. Notice what you got back
    Did it say it didn’t know? Hedge? Answer with confidence? Either way, notice whether you’d have any way to tell a confident wrong answer from a confident right one, just from reading it.
  3. Get the real document
    Download the 2025 Graduate Program Requirements PDF from the link above — this is the live, official, currently-in-effect document, straight from UIC’s CS department.
  4. Ground it, and ask the exact same question again
    Attach the PDF to your AI assistant (or paste in the relevant section if it can’t take file uploads). Ask the identical question from step 1. Compare the two answers side by side, and ask it to quote the exact sentence each part of the answer came from.

Worth noticing

This is a real, currently-live UIC policy document — but it’s also narrow and specific in a way most general AI training data won’t reliably cover. That’s the point: most of what you’ll ever need to fact-check in real life is exactly this kind of thing — official, findable, but too niche or too specific for any AI to have reliably learned it during training. Grounding isn’t optional polish for those cases. It’s the whole ballgame.

Beat Two — Extract it, check it

Goal: pull exact facts out of running text and a table — then use that skill to catch a realistic mix-up.

  1. Learn the technique: quote, then interpret
    For any fact you care about, ask your grounded assistant for two things separately: the exact source text it’s relying on (a verbatim quote), and its interpretation of that text. Put them side by side — if the interpretation claims more than the quote actually supports, that’s your signal.
  2. Pull two facts, two formats
    From the PDF (running text): get the exact committee composition required for a PhD Preliminary Exam. From the catalog page (a table): get the minimum TOEFL iBT score for admission — and check whether it depends on when the test was taken.
  3. Now check a claim someone made to Priya
    A friend who already has a master’s degree told her: “You’ll need at least 32 hours of coursework before you can start on your PhD research at UIC.” Using your grounded assistant, figure out whether that’s accurate — and if not, where the real number actually comes from.
  4. Don’t stop at right or wrong
    If the claim turns out to be off, figure out why it’s a plausible thing to have said — is there a real number nearby that it could be confused with? That habit is what actually prevents the next mix-up.

If you think you've found it

Don’t just flag that a number seems off — pin down exactly what the correct figure is, which exact sentence proves it, and why the other number is also real (just for a different situation). Nothing here was planted as a trick; it’s two genuinely similar, genuinely real requirements that are easy to swap.

Beat Three — Summarize it, verify that too

Goal: answer a question with no single verbatim sentence to check it against — and figure out how you’d verify it anyway.

  1. Ask a synthesis question
    Ask your grounded assistant: “Summarize, in a few sentences, everything a student entering with a master’s degree needs to accomplish, in order, from admission through their final defense, with roughly when each milestone should happen.”
  2. Trace the answer back
    For each part of the answer, ask yourself (or your assistant): what specific section does this trace back to? Flag anything the answer adds that you can’t trace to any section at all.
  3. Triangulate
    Ask the same question again with different wording. If part of the answer only shows up with one specific phrasing, treat that part as suspect.
  4. Check for the common shortcut
    A weak summary often turns the colloquium and CS 590 requirements into a sequential step instead of something running in parallel with the qualifier and coursework. Check whether your answer did the same thing.

Why Beat Three doesn’t work like Beat Two

Beat Two had one right answer sitting in one place — you could check it against a quote. Beat Three’s answer is assembled from several sections at once, so there’s no single sentence to diff against. Verifying synthesis takes more judgment, not less — that’s true even when the assistant is perfectly grounded.

Two things to leave with

  • 1

    The searching got easy. The checking is the actual skill.

    Using AI search well is mostly the work of verifying what it surfaces, not the work of searching itself — and that work is invisible if you don’t know to look for it.

  • 2

    Watch for what it can't reach, not just what it gets wrong

    Over-relying on AI search trains you to stop looking anywhere it can’t reach — the department office, an actual advisor, anything too recent or too niche to be indexed. You stop knowing what you don’t know, because the tool never shows you the shape of its own blind spot.

A note on the real document: UIC updates these requirements from time to time. Keep your answers from today if you can, but if you (or anyone you know) ever need a real answer about real degree requirements, always re-check the live PDF and talk to an actual advisor — this module is a literacy exercise, not official advising.
