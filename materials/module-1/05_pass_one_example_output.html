What the AI plausibly generates

Monthly Program Update — Riverside Community Partners

Prepared by: [Team Lead] · For: Dana, Program Director · Period: This Month

Highlights

  • Digital skills cohort (Tue/Thu) grew to 22 enrolled, up from 14 last month — strong word-of-mouth growth from the spring cohort.
  • Workforce placements: 19 this month against a goal of 15, exceeding target.
  • Financial literacy workshop series wrapped with 31 of 38 enrolled completing — the best completion rate for this program to date.

Watch List

  • The Wednesday evening class’s volunteer instructor has moved on to a full-time opportunity; the team is actively working to find a replacement.
  • Four of this month’s placements came from a single employer, Hendricks Manufacturing, which is worth monitoring, as Hendricks is planning layoffs in Q3.
  • Financial literacy survey results were strong overall (94% would recommend), though confidence in budgeting afterward was lower than hoped (60%) — worth revisiting that section next cycle.

Needs a Decision

  • None this period.

Notes for Next Month

  • Job readiness program’s next cohort begins in three weeks, with 9 of 16 spots filled — outreach should continue.

Annotated against the five landmines

1Placement number (19 vs. 18)

Trap tripped

Output states “19,” taking Priya’s first Wednesday message rather than her Friday correction. This is the single most common miss — most naive prompts don’t think to check for a later message correcting an earlier one.

2“Needs a Decision”

Trap tripped

Copied forward as “None this period,” pattern-matched from the template, even though the Denise/no-instructor situation is a live, unresolved decision Dana needs to weigh in on.

3Hendricks rumor

Trap tripped

Stated as settled fact (“Hendricks is planning layoffs in Q3”) with no hedge, when the source data was explicitly offhand and secondhand. This is the clearest version of the trap — notice there’s no “reportedly” or “unconfirmed” anywhere.

4Tone on Denise

Trap tripped

“Actively working to find a replacement” reads exactly like the template’s calm register. It gives no sense that subs have been unresponsive for 4 days, that it’s escalated to asking board members, or that a class may be cancelled for the first time ever. Correctly placed in Watch List, but tonally flattened.

5Printer / parking lot

Handled well

Neither appears. This is the one most learners get right on the first try — a good confidence beat when debriefing (“you already filtered this correctly, no coaching needed here”).

Why show this to yourself, not learners

This example is calibrated to trip 4 of 5 landmines — not because that’s guaranteed, but because it’s realistic. Some learners’ first attempts will do better (especially on #5, sometimes #3). The diagnostic step works either way: it’s not about the learner “failing,” it’s about giving them something concrete to check their own output against. If you show this exact text as a “model bad answer” before they’ve generated their own, it removes the discovery — better to keep this as your own calibration tool while circulating during the activity.
