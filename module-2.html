AI Literacy 101 · Module 2

Scattered Sources

From Data Dump to Decision-Ready: turning a messy folder of spreadsheets into a report you can defend, with AI as assistant or analyst.

90 minutes Level 1 Who it’s for: everyone

TL;DR

You’ll work the same messy, undocumented spreadsheet folder twice — once using AI as an assistant to get oriented, once letting AI act as an analyst and generate the report directly. Same data, two very different levels of trust and verification.

Prerequisites Computer literacy. Module 1 recommended but not required — this module reuses its vocabulary (uncover your goal, one step at a time, don’t outsource your thinking).
What you’ll need A laptop with internet access, spreadsheet software (Excel or Google Sheets), and access to an AI assistant (ChatGPT or Claude).
Who it’s for Everyone — no technical background required.

After this module, you’ll be able to

  • 1 Get oriented before you generate — use AI to make sense of messy, undocumented data before asking it to produce anything.
  • 2 Hand AI more autonomy, deliberately — know when you’re asking AI to assist vs. to analyze, and verify accordingly.
  • 3 Catch what AI gets wrong with real spreadsheets that contradict themselves — the kind your actual job produces.

Preparation

  • 1 Read Module2_Facilitator_Manual.html in full, especially the Data Notes section — fastest way to get oriented without opening all six source files yourself.
  • 2 Skim the deck’s speaker notes for timing and beat-by-beat guidance.
  • 3 Memorize the number chain that answers most diagnostic checks: $261,500 (correct Q3 2023 baseline) × 1.15 (FY2024 growth target) = $300,725 (Q3 2024 target) — with the Ottawa CAD pilot excluded throughout.
  • 4 Nothing to print. Have the Q3 data workbook and the Q2 check-in deck open in separate windows, ready to share-screen.
  • 5 Confirm learners have spreadsheet software available — both beats require opening an actual multi-tab workbook, not just reading text.
